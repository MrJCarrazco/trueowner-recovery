Surplus Funds Recovery

A government agency may be holding money that belongs to you.

When a property is sold at a tax or foreclosure sale for more than what was owed, the extra money — the surplus — belongs to the former owner. Counties and states hold millions of dollars of it, waiting to be claimed. We help rightful owners get it back.

What are surplus funds?

When a county sells a property at a tax sale or foreclosure sale, the sale often brings in more money than the taxes or debt that were owed. By law, that extra amount does not belong to the county, and it does not belong to the buyer. It belongs to the former owner of the property — or to their heirs.

The problem: the government agency holding the money is only required to send a notice to the owner's last known address. People move. Mail goes astray. Families don't know a parent or grandparent ever owned the property. So the money sits in a government account — sometimes for years — until someone claims it.

This is real, verifiable money held by real government offices. Any county clerk or treasurer can confirm whether they are holding surplus funds connected to a property. We encourage you to verify everything we tell you directly with the government office involved — we will give you their public phone number.
