About the funds

Is this real? It sounds too good to be true.

Healthy skepticism is exactly right — this niche attracts scammers, which is why we build verification into our process. The funds are real: when a property sells at a tax or foreclosure sale for more than what was owed, most state laws require the surplus to be held for the former owner. Any county clerk or treasurer can confirm whether they hold surplus funds for a given property. Before you sign anything with us, we will tell you which government office holds the funds and give you their public phone number so you can check for yourself.

Why didn't the government just send me the money?

Agencies are generally required only to mail a notice to the owner's last known address — often the address of the property that was just sold. If you moved, never got the notice, or inherited the right to the funds without knowing it, the money simply sits unclaimed. Some states eventually transfer it to a state unclaimed property fund; it can sit there for years more.

How did you find me?

Public records only: government surplus lists, property records, and people-search databases built from public information. We never buy private data, access credit reports, or use anything that isn't publicly available.

How much money is it?

We share the specifics — the holding office, the property, and what the records show — in a direct conversation after we've confirmed we're speaking with the right person. We don't print dollar amounts in first letters, for your protection: a letter delivered to a wrong address with a dollar figure on it invites someone else to attempt a fraudulent claim on your money.

About the fee

What does it cost?

Nothing up front. Nothing at all unless you receive your funds. Our fee is a percentage of what you actually recover — up to 30% depending on your state's limits. Several states cap recovery-assistance fees below 30% by law; where they do, we charge at or below the legal cap. The exact percentage for your claim is written in your agreement before you sign.

Who gets paid first?

You do. The agency pays the funds to you — not to us. We invoice our fee only after the money has actually reached you. If the claim fails, you owe nothing.

Can I claim the funds myself and skip the fee?

Yes. You may be able to claim these funds on your own without paying anyone. Our service is optional — and we're required in several states to tell you so, which we'd do anyway. Each agency has its own claim process you can pursue directly. People hire us because the research, documentation, deadlines, and follow-up take real time and care — and because with us, all the risk of a failed claim is ours, not yours.

About trusting us

How do I know you're not a scam?

Test us against the warning signs consumer-protection offices publish:

  • Upfront fees? We never charge one. Anyone who does is a red flag.
  • Pressure and deadlines? We don't pressure. Take the time you need.
  • Secrecy? We tell you exactly which government office holds the funds and encourage you to call them yourself before signing anything.
  • Sensitive data up front? We never ask for your Social Security number, bank login, or payment details in a first conversation.

And our agreement states, in writing, that you can claim on your own and that you pay nothing unless you receive your funds.

Will you need my personal documents?

Eventually, yes — government agencies require proof of identity and proof of your connection to the former property before they release funds (that's a good thing; it protects your money). We request those documents only after you've signed an agreement, and only the documents the agency itself requires.

The former owner was my parent or grandparent, who has passed away. Can the family still claim?

Usually, yes — the right to surplus funds generally passes to heirs. These claims need extra documentation (and in some states a court step), which is exactly the kind of paperwork we manage. Where an attorney is required, we work with licensed attorneys.

Are you a law firm or a government agency?

No. We are a private research and recovery-assistance service. We don't give legal advice, and we're not affiliated with any government office. Where a claim requires court filings or legal representation, we work with licensed attorneys rather than pretending to be one.
