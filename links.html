Start with your county

What surplus funds are

When a property is sold at a tax sale, the sale can bring in more money than the taxes, penalties and costs that were owed. That extra amount is commonly called surplus funds, excess proceeds or an overage.

Depending on the law and the recorded interests involved, that money may belong to the former owner or their heirs. It is not automatic, and every case turns on its own records.

Where the money is held

The money is usually held by the county or public office connected with the sale — often a treasurer's office, clerk of court or court registry.

Start by identifying the county, the property and the sale date, then ask that office for its official instructions. States and counties use different forms, waiting periods and deadlines.

What a claim generally involves

A claim generally starts with the sale record. The claimant usually has to show who they are and document their connection to the property that was sold.

Then the claim goes to the office holding the money, using that office's forms and supporting-document rules, followed by review before anything is released.

Verify before you act

Use the government office's own published instructions and confirm deadlines directly with that office. Records can be incomplete, and other ownership or lien interests may affect a claim.

Be cautious with anyone who demands money up front or guarantees an outcome. No honest educational summary can promise that funds exist or will be paid in a particular case.
