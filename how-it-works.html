The Process

From first call to funds in your hands.

Five steps, fully explained, no surprises — and no cost to you unless the recovery succeeds.

Where the money comes from

Counties regularly sell properties at public auction to collect unpaid property taxes, or through foreclosure. Bidders often pay far more than the amount of taxes or debt owed. After the government takes what it is owed, the law in most states says the remainder — the surplus, sometimes called excess proceeds or overage — must be held for the former owner.

In 2023 the U.S. Supreme Court (Tyler v. Hennepin County) confirmed what most state laws already said: that leftover equity belongs to the former owner, not the government. But the money doesn't deliver itself. Someone has to claim it, with the right paperwork, at the right office, within the deadlines each state sets.

The five steps

1

We find the funds and find you

We research county and state surplus lists, property records, and public records to identify funds and locate the people with the right to claim them. If we contacted you, this step is already done.

2

Free review — and independent verification

We explain what we found: which government office holds the funds and what property they come from. We'll give you that office's public phone number so you can confirm everything yourself before deciding anything. This conversation costs nothing and commits you to nothing.

3

A plain-language agreement

If you want our help, we put the arrangement in writing: the service, the exact fee percentage (up to 30%, lower where your state's law caps it lower), and the fact that you owe nothing if you don't receive funds. You keep a copy. Take your time — have a lawyer look at it if you wish.

4

We document and manage the claim

We gather the required proof — identity, former ownership, heirship documents if the owner has passed away — prepare the paperwork, submit it to the right office, and respond to whatever the agency asks for, keeping you informed at every step. Where a state requires an attorney for part of the process, we work with licensed attorneys.

5

You get paid — then we do

The agency pays the funds to you. After the money has actually reached you, we invoice our agreed fee. If the claim fails for any reason, you owe us nothing.

How long does it take?

It varies by state and county — typically a few weeks to several months from signed agreement to payment, depending on the office's workload, the completeness of the records, and whether a court has to approve the disbursement. We'll give you an honest estimate for your specific claim, and honest updates while it's pending.

You may be able to claim these funds on your own without paying anyone. Our service is optional. Every holding agency has a claim process you can pursue yourself. What we sell is the research that found the funds, the experience to assemble a claim correctly the first time, and the fact that we carry all the risk — we are paid only out of a successful recovery.
